Plain-language glossary

Trichinella
A parasite whose larvae can live in pork muscle. Curing, heating and freezing can destroy the larvae.
Striated pork muscle
Ordinary skeletal muscle, the "meat" in pork. The CFIA methods are written for products that contain it.
Curing
Treating meat with salt (and other curing materials) for a set time and temperature, often followed by drying or smoking.
Drying room
A room held at a minimum temperature where cured product loses moisture over days or weeks.
Overhaul
Turning cured pieces over and applying more cure to cover the lean meat.
Cure equalization
A rest period after curing that lets the salt spread deeper into the meat.
Salimeter
A scale that shows how strong a salt pickle (brine) is.
Thermocouple
A temperature probe placed in the product (or freezer) and connected to a recording thermometer.
Lot
The group of product treated and recorded together.
Uninterrupted
The time count restarts if the freezer temperature rises above the required temperature.

About this tool

This tool follows the CFIA page Control recommendations for the inactivation of Trichinella spiralis in pork products (page last modified 2022-10-07). Check the page for the latest wording, since it may change.

It is a learning and planning aid, not a regulatory decision or a validation. Where a rule on the page is unclear or looks inconsistent, the tool says so and suggests confirming with CFIA.
