Fermented and Dried Meat Control Finder

A guide to the CFIA controls for fermented and dried meat products, other than Staphylococcus degree-hours (which has its own calculator). It covers E. coli and Salmonella control in fermented sausage, dried products, shelf stability and C. botulinum. Where the CFIA page leaves room for judgment, the calculators take the more cautious reading.

Plain-language glossary

Fermented sausage (dry or semi-dry)
Sausage made with a starter culture or natural fermentation that lowers the pH, then usually dried, such as salami or pepperoni.
Log reduction (for example "5-log")
Each 1-log reduction kills 90% of the bacteria. A 5-log reduction cuts them by a factor of 100,000, and a 2-log reduction by 99%.
Validated process
A process shown by scientific evidence, such as a challenge study, to achieve a stated log reduction.
Challenge study
A laboratory test in which meat is deliberately inoculated with the bacteria, run through the process, and counted again at the end.
Lot
The group of product sampled and judged together. CFIA limits a lot to one day's production.
Composite sample
Up to three samples combined into one for analysis.
Lethality process
A heat (or other kill) step shown to reduce bacteria by a set number of logs.
Water activity (aw)
A measure of how much water in a food is available to microbes. Lower values slow microbial growth.
Moisture:protein ratio
A measure of how dry a sausage is. Lower means drier.

How the calculators stay on the cautious side

  • Strict comparisons. A condition written "more than 6 days", "pH below 4.6" or "pH above 5.0" is treated strictly. Equal does not qualify.
  • Exact process match. The published validated processes were tested at specific conditions, so the chamber temperature must match one of them. The tool never interpolates between rows.
  • Casing size gap. The tables list "under 55 mm" and "56 to 105 mm". A casing of 55 to under 56 mm is treated as the larger category, which has the more demanding heat schedules.
  • Heat schedules. Each required stage must be matched by its own stage of your schedule, in order, with the time and temperature at least as high. Temperatures are treated as product temperatures, which is the cautious reading since the page specifies this for only one row.
  • Error allowance. The temperature error you enter (default 1.0 °C) is subtracted from heat readings before they are compared with the tables, as CFIA expects limits to be adjusted by equipment error.
  • Table 2 temperatures. For each row, the stricter of the page's °C value and the exact conversion of its °F value is used (for example 137 °F is 58.33 °C, but the page lists 58.4 °C).
  • Between table rows. The next lowest row is used, never an interpolated value, and above the hottest row the 4-minute row applies.
  • Challenge study results. The study is judged on its weakest replicate, using the lower starting count and the higher final count. A final count reported as "less than" a detection limit counts only as a minimum reduction.
  • Release and disposition. Any positive result means hold. Missing results or an incomplete sample set also means hold. Release is possible only when every test is complete and negative.
  • Unsure answers. If you are not sure whether a plant condition applies, the tool treats it as applying.

About this tool

This tool follows the CFIA page Preventive control recommendations for manufacturing fermented and dried meat products (page modified 2020-03-05). Check the page for the latest wording. It is a learning and planning aid, not a validation or a regulatory decision. Where the page is unclear, the tool says so and suggests confirming with CFIA.
