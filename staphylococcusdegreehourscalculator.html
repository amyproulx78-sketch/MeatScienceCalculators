Staphylococcus Degree-Hours Calculator

For fermented meat products. Check whether a fermentation reached pH 5.3 within the CFIA degree-hours limit for Staphylococcus aureus, decide what to do with a lot that did not, and run two related checks. Where the CFIA page leaves room for judgment, this tool takes the more cautious reading.

Step 1: Fermentation Temperatures

Degree-hours count from the start of fermentation until the product reaches a pH of 5.3. Use surface temperature if you can measure it, otherwise fermentation room temperature.

How this tool stays on the cautious side

  • Strictly "fewer than". CFIA's limits are "fewer than" 665, 555 or 500 degree-hours. A result equal to the limit fails.
  • Error allowance. The temperature error you enter is added to every reading, since warmer fermentation means more degree-hours. The default is 1.0 °C, the accuracy CFIA recommends for monitoring equipment on its cooked ready-to-eat page. The result without the allowance is shown too, which is how the page calculates.
  • Limit set by the highest temperature. The page bases the limit on the highest temperature reached before pH 5.3, even if only briefly. A temperature of exactly 33 °C or 37 °C uses the 555 band, as in the page's table.
  • Below 15.6 °C counts as zero. Cooler time adds no degree-hours and is never subtracted.
  • Logger data. Each interval uses the higher of its two readings, and the reading just after the pH time is included when finding the highest temperature.
  • Constant-temperature runs. At a temperature listed in CFIA's Table 1, the hours must meet both the formula and the table, whichever is stricter. The table is slightly stricter than the formula at the cool end (for example 150.0 hours against 151.1 at 20 °C) and slightly looser at 30 °C and 40 °C, where its listed hours (46.2 and 20.5) give 665.3 and 500.2 degree-hours, just over the limits.
  • pH not reached. If the product has not reached pH 5.3, the lot is held, because growth and toxin formation can continue.
  • Disposition. A count of exactly 104 per gram is treated as "higher than 104". Incomplete testing means hold. Any enterotoxin means destroy. Detection of another pathogen means do not sell.
  • Narrow margins are flagged. A result at 90% or more of the limit is marked as close, because small measurement errors could change the answer. This warning is the tool's own, not CFIA's.
  • pH record. The pH values you record never change the degree-hours or the verdict. They are used only to cross-check the time counted. If the record shows pH 5.3 was reached later than the time counted, the result is flagged as possibly too low. If it was reached earlier, the extra time is still counted.
  • One batch is not a process. CFIA judges a process acceptable if product consistently reaches pH 5.3 within the limit. Keep records for every lot.

Source: CFIA, Preventive control recommendations for manufacturing fermented and dried meat products (page modified 2020-03-05). Check the page for the latest wording. This is a learning tool, not a validation.
