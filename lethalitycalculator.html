Notes and limitations

  • CFIA tables. The values come from the CFIA page Preventive control recommendations for manufacturing cooked ready-to-eat meat products (page modified 2025-05-29), which says they are derived from the US FSIS Appendix A guidelines. Check the current CFIA page, since it may change. The target is a 6.5-log reduction of Salmonella with no poultry, and 7.0-log with poultry.
  • How it works. Each reading earns a fraction of the required hold time for the temperature it was at (interpolated between table rows). When the fractions add up to 100%, the target is met. Below the lowest temperature in the table nothing is counted. At or above the temperature where the table shows a hold under 10 seconds, the target is treated as met instantly.
  • Fat content. Fat is rounded up to the next whole percent, and anything under 1% uses the 1% column. The CFIA tables stop at 12% fat, so above that a validated alternative from a Process Authority is needed.
  • Measurement error. CFIA expects limits to be adjusted by the equipment's stated error and recommends equipment accurate to ±1.0 °C. The error you enter is subtracted from every reading.
  • D and Z method. Lethality is added up with the trapezoid rule, the same approach as the NAMI spreadsheet. Log reduction is the F-value divided by D.
  • Data quality. The NAMI spreadsheet asks for at least 20 data points. Because lethality rises steeply with temperature, long gaps between readings during heating tend to overestimate lethality.
  • Not a validation. Probe placement at the true coldest spot, product formulation and the rest of the preventive control plan all matter. A real process must be validated by a qualified Process Authority. This tool is for learning.
