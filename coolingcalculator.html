Notes and limitations

  • Source of the rules. The schedules come from the CFIA page Preventive control recommendations for cooling heat processed meat products (page dated 2018-06-13). It also cites US FSIS Appendix B (1999) cooling parameters, which are included as separate options. Check the current CFIA page, since it may change.
  • What "cooling started" means. This tool treats the hottest reading as the start of cooling. Zone times are measured between the first times the temperature falls to each limit, with straight-line interpolation between readings. If your data begins below an upper limit, the zone is assumed to start at the first reading.
  • Measurement error. CFIA expects limits to be adjusted by the equipment's stated error and recommends equipment accurate to ±1.0 °C. Because a warm product is the worse case in cooling, the error you enter is added to every reading.
  • What this tool cannot check. Surface temperature records, protection from post-cooking contamination, when chilling began after the cook, and the product conditions you tick in Step 1 are on your honour. A real plant must confirm them.
  • It does not predict bacterial growth. CFIA says a cooling deviation should be assessed with predictive modelling using worst-case inputs, and the product may only be considered for release if the model predicts no more than a 1-log CFU/g increase of C. perfringens and no growth of C. botulinum. End-product testing is also expected, but a negative test alone is not a basis for release.
  • Not a validation. This is a learning tool. Real cooling schedules must be validated and approved by a qualified Process Authority.
